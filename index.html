Interior Design

How we work

Scroll

Interiors that work as hard as they look — spatial planning, materiality, light and acoustics shaped around the people who will actually use the space.

Interior design at MG Design Engineering begins with a simple question: how will this space really be used? From that answer we build a brief that balances atmosphere with operation — so the finished interior supports daily life, reinforces the identity of the place, and holds its value long after handover.

Every decision is tested against how the space will be used, operated, and maintained.

We work across workplace, hospitality, residential, and cultural interiors — from early concept sketches and spatial strategy through to FF&E selection, technical documentation, and on-site review. Each stage is designed to reduce risk and sharpen the brief, so clients can commit with confidence rather than assumption.

Materiality is chosen for how it ages, not just how it photographs. Lighting is layered for task, mood and wayfinding. Acoustics are treated as a design material in their own right. And every layout is pressure-tested against circulation, storage, and the realities of day-to-day operation.

The result is interiors that feel considered from the moment you step inside — and that continue to perform years later, when the novelty has worn off and the space has to earn its keep.

Architecture

How we design

Scroll

Buildings that hold their ground — form, structure and material resolved against site, climate, and the way people will actually move through them.

Architecture at MG Design Engineering begins with the site and the brief in equal measure. We read orientation, access, context and constraint before a line is drawn — so the building that emerges is shaped by where it stands and who it serves, not by a style applied after the fact.

A building earns its place when it works for the city, the client, and the people inside it.

We work across residential, workplace, cultural, and mixed-use buildings — from feasibility and concept design through to technical documentation, tender, and on-site review. Each stage is structured to reduce risk and sharpen the design, so clients can commit with confidence rather than assumption.

Structure and services are treated as part of the architecture, not a layer applied beneath it. Environmental strategy — daylight, thermal performance, ventilation — is tested from the earliest massing studies. Materials are chosen for how they weather, not just how they render.

The result is buildings that feel inevitable on their site — and that continue to perform decades later, when the drawings have long been filed away and the building has to stand on its own.

Urban Design

How we shape cities

Scroll

Cities are made of movement, edge conditions, and the spaces between buildings — urban design is the discipline of getting those relationships right before the buildings arrive.

Urban design at MG Design Engineering works at the scale where architecture meets infrastructure. We begin with how people, goods and water move through a site, then shape blocks, streets, public spaces and density around that movement — so the masterplan is legible long before it is built, and stays usable long after.

A good masterplan is one that keeps working when the first generation of buildings is replaced.

We work across district masterplans, transit-oriented developments, mixed-use quarters and public realm strategies — from site and context analysis through to design codes, phasing frameworks, and delivery guidance. Every stage is tested against viability and public benefit, not just aesthetics.

Movement is treated as the first design move. Streets, blocks and connections are sized for how people actually travel — walking, cycling, transit, service — and public space is distributed where it does the most work for the most people. Density is calibrated to infrastructure, daylight and the character of the surrounding context.

The result is urban frameworks that read clearly on a plan, work economically on a spreadsheet, and feel generous at eye level — the three tests every masterplan has to pass to be worth building.
